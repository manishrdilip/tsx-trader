# Billion campaign launch readiness

Status: **not started**. The official ledger target is 1,000,000,000 and the official count is zero. The local runner is limited to engineering milestones until production accounting and the launch gates below are implemented and verified.

## Verified locally

- Daily fills use a close signal, next-session open entry, and an open-to-open hold of 2, 5, or 10 observed bars. Positions use whole units, $5,000 starting cash, 0.05% cost per side, and no overlap. Each split starts flat; positions cannot cross splits. The engine reports closed-trade drawdown and records accrued wins, losses and count for expectancy and profit-factor calculations.
- Seven Python feature families are wired into the compiled daily evaluation. Candidate IDs encode one predicate per selected family, fixed exact depth, threshold/window and holding period. Affine permutation over the bounded space deterministically samples without replacement. Five-family depth has 2,113,929,216 canonical configurations before data and execution are considered.
- Engineering dates are aligned at 2016-01-01 (train), 2022-01-01 (validation), and 2025-01-01 (holdout). The evaluator excludes holdout rows from the query output and does not calculate holdout metrics. This is an engineering split, not evidence that the dates are independent or that an access-controlled lockbox exists.
- CPU benchmarks exclude JIT warm-up and include Zstandard Parquet output. SHOP.TO 10k was 32,540 evaluations/s with one thread and 78,867 with eight threads, with identical results. XEG.TO 100k was 100,874 evaluations/s on eight threads. AAPL.US 1M uninterrupted was 111,124 evaluations/s and wrote 54,517,149 bytes. At the observed 1M storage density, a billion rows would occupy about 51 GiB of Parquet before ledger and operational overhead. Short workloads do not establish sustained billion-run throughput, peak memory, or thermals.
- A forced Windows process termination after 25,000 committed evaluations was recovered by the standalone watchdog. Forty output batches matched the uninterrupted 1M run byte-for-byte, and DuckDB independently counted 1M unique IDs. An operating-system lock prevents concurrent writers on one job; checksummed committed batches are reconciled before resume and a leased tail is retried.
- The dashboard is loopback-only. The watchdog reports disk, available RAM, and GPU temperature when readable. The laptop exposes no usable CPU thermal reading through the tested interface. CUDA was unavailable to the tested runtime, so no GPU performance claim is made.
- A deterministic set of 64 strategies was replayed from committed Parquet and checked against the stored metrics. Local audit evidence includes decoded rules and dated-split trade indices. The allocation validator separately passed exact-billion and repeated-scope rejection tests using mock fingerprints.
- Added 17 EODHD histories. The warehouse now has 286,211 daily bars in 40 series, with a fresh local calendar audit. Source data and detailed test results remain on D:.

## Engineering entry points

The isolated venv contains NumPy, Numba, pandas, DuckDB and PyArrow. With the configured daily revision manifest under `D:\UniversalQuantLab\config`, use `daily_campaign.py --help` for direct execution, `campaign_watchdog.py ROOT JOB_JSON` for a standalone process, and `campaign_dashboard.py RUN_DIRECTORY --port 8765` for loopback monitoring. Create a `PAUSE` marker inside the run directory to stop at a checkpoint. Clear the marker after reviewing the resource or operator pause and rerun with the same manifest.

`campaign_progress.py` checks completed ledger rows, hashes, and unique strategy IDs before writing a small engineering summary. Its optional `--publish` uses an authenticated local GitHub CLI and updates only the designated progress JSON path. It never sends Parquet or licensed market data.
